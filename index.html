How to use this

  • Read one domain in Study, then immediately drill that domain in Practice. Loop.
  • Flashcards are for the vocabulary and the numbers — job names, config keys, EVENTSOURCE values, ranks.
  • Mock exam only once you are above ~75% in practice. It draws to the true 15/15/10/10/15/5 weighting.
  • Use the SailPoint bridge to anchor new terms, then drop it. The exam tests Saviynt’s object model directly.

The mindset that passes

Think in flows and relationships, not isolated features. Identity data lands in the warehouse. Access is requested through ARS. Rules automate lifecycle outcomes. SoD checks whether combinations are toxic. Attestation reviews what exists. Analytics reports what happened.

On an ambiguous scenario question, first identify which domain it belongs to. That alone usually eliminates half the options.

Two conflicting passing scores exist in official material. The current Exam Preparation Guide states a 50–500 scale with a minimum passing score of 350. The older Certification Program FAQ PDF still says “70%”. Treat the Exam Preparation Guide as authoritative for the current scoring model. This app scores mocks on the 50–500 scale and also shows your raw percentage so you can sanity-check yourself against both.
Question-type wording quirk. The guide says “there are two types of questions” and then lists three (multiple choice, multiple response, scenario based). Its own sample question 1 is a True/False item. Expect True/False to appear in practice even though it is not named in the type list — this app includes them.

Compensatory scoring — what it actually means

You do not need to pass each domain individually. Your total scaled score is what decides pass or fail. That has a practical consequence: the two 15-question domains (Building Identity Warehouse and ARS) plus Attestation are 64% of the exam between them. Analytics is only 5 questions — worth knowing cold because it is small, but never worth more study time than ARS.

Your score report shows per-domain performance bands, which is why this hub tracks accuracy per domain rather than just an overall number.
